# Dream of North

### No race number, no tracker, no cut-off. Just 71°10′ N and whatever the road does in between.

There are ideas that show up once, get politely filed away, and never come back. And then there are the other kind — the ones that sit in the back of your head for two years, quietly rearranging everything else around themselves.

Mine started as a race entry. North Cape 4000. Number on the bike, clock running, Nordkapp at the end of it. I planned for it, trained for it, and somewhere along the way I noticed I was more interested in the map than in the result.

**So I kept the map and threw away the clock.**

The route is drawn. It leaves my driveway in Königswinter and ends on a cliff at 71°10′ north. 4,243 kilometres on paper, about 4,040 of them actually pedalled — the rest is boats. 15,800 metres of climbing. Three countries, two ferries, one bike. Somewhere between 21 and 28 days, depending on what the wind and I agree on that morning.

The start is summer 2028. Everything between now and then is preparation.

## The first thousand: getting out of Germany

The opening leg is the least romantic and, I suspect, the most important. Rhine, then north-west through the Ruhr, Münsterland, Osnabrück, the Weser, the long flat run over the Elbe and up through Holstein. Roughly 880 km to the Danish border. No mountains, no drama, no scenery to hide behind.

This is where the trip is actually decided. Not on some Norwegian pass — here, on day four, on a farm track in the rain, when the daily rhythm either locks in or it doesn't. If I can hold 150 to 200 km a day through Lower Saxony without wrecking myself, the rest is just more of the same with better views.

## Denmark, and the first sea

Jutland north to Hirtshals: another 420 km of wind and gravel. Denmark has a habit of handing out shelters in the middle of nowhere, which suits a trip with no fixed nights booked.

Then the Skagerrak — 155 km of ferry across to Norway, the one place on the route where the line on the map stops being mine. **Km 1,300. From here it's Norway all the way.**

## Norway: 2,700 kilometres of it

This is the part that doesn't fit into a paragraph, so I'll let the numbers do it.

- **Km 2,860** — the Arctic Circle. 66°34′ N. After this, the light stops behaving normally.
- **Km 3,230** — Lofoten. White sand, turquoise water, a ferry hop, and — if I'm honest with myself — more campervans than I'd like.
- **Km 3,675** — Tromsø. Last real city. Last proper bike shop.
- **Km 4,243** — Nordkapp.

The last 500 km are the ones people warn you about: headwind, reindeer, tunnels, and long stretches where the next shop is a planning problem rather than a stop.

## What the route is, and what it isn't

I want to be straight about this, because "gravel to the North Cape" makes a better headline than it does a description.

15,800 metres of climbing across 4,000 km is flat for Norway. This is a valley-and-coast line, built to be ridden fast and loaded, not a fjell traverse. There will be gravel — a lot of it in Denmark and on the Norwegian side roads — but there will also be plenty of tarmac and a fair share of E6. Anyone who wants 30,000 metres of climbing and 60 % unpaved should draw a different line.

What it is instead: a route I can hold at 150–200 km a day for three or four weeks without turning the whole thing into suffering as an end in itself.

## Why not just ride the race

Because the race gives you a route _and_ a clock, and I only wanted one of them.

No number means I can stop in Lofoten for two days if Lofoten deserves two days. No tracker means nobody is watching me make a bad decision at 22:00 in the rain. No cut-off means the trip is finished when I'm standing on the cliff, not when a rule says so.

It isn't harder than the NC4000. It's just mine.

## Between now and 2028

Two years is a long runway, and I intend to use all of it:

✅ Staged prep tours — long weekends first, then a full week at target pace, then a two-week block loaded exactly as I'll ride it ✅ The setup decided early and then left alone — light, front-and-frame, no panniers, no last-minute experiments ✅ Nordic supply planning practised somewhere less remote than Finnmark ✅ Everything re-planned once as a gravel route, just to see honestly what it costs in metres and days

And the one thing I already know I'll carry, because everyone who has ridden north says the same: a spare shift cable.

**Dream of North.** Might be the one last big experiment. Or the second last.
